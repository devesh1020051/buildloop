Industrial 6-axis arm

Ready
35 deg

Six servos, wired like a kit. Each servo angle (0 to 180) maps to a joint: D3 base turn = (servo minus 90) times 1.8 degrees. D5 shoulder elevation = servo minus 45 degrees above horizontal. D6 elbow bend = servo minus 150 degrees, so the forearm elevation is shoulder plus elbow. D9 forearm roll = (servo minus 90) times 2. D10 wrist pitch = (servo minus 90) times 1.3 degrees. D11 tool roll = (servo minus 90) times 2. Suction pump on D8. Upper arm 140 mm, forearm 130 mm, shoulder 100 mm above the table, tool 80 mm long. +x is right, +y is away from you, z is up. Blocks are 50 by 25 by 25 mm.

SCARA arm

Ready
0 deg

Wired like a robot kit. Shoulder servo on D3: the servo angle is the shoulder angle in degrees from the +x axis (0 to 180, so 90 points straight ahead). Elbow servo on D5: the elbow bend is (servo angle minus 90) times 1.5 degrees, because of a 1.5 gear step-up. Z servo on D6: 0 puts the tip on the table and 180 lifts it 50 mm. Suction pump on D8. Delivery robot (AMR)

Go to pickup

Wired like a robot kit. Motors: ENA 5, IN1 7, IN2 8 and ENB 6, IN3 9, IN4 10. Range sensors in 2 mm steps: A0 front (a narrow cone), A1 left and A2 right (beams angled 50 degrees out), up to 1 m. A3: battery, 0 to 1023 for 0 to 100 %. A4: bearing to the current target, 512 straight ahead and about 2.84 counts per degree, bigger when it is to the left. A5: distance to the current target in 2 mm steps. D2 reads 1 while carrying the parcel and D3 reads 1 once it is delivered. The target is the pickup, then the drop-off, then the dock.

Quadcopter drone

On the pad
11 V

Four 3-phase BLDC motors, each on its own ESC. The flight controller sends each ESC a servo pulse: front-left on pin 3, front-right on pin 5, back-left on pin 6 and back-right on pin 9 (the pad names printed on the board depend on the flight controller you pick in the Board list). Use the Servo library and writeMicroseconds: 1000 us is stopped, 2000 us is full power, and each ESC needs a second at 1000 us to arm. The two diagonal pairs spin opposite ways, and the spin direction depends on the order of the three phase wires. Sensors are on the flight controller board. Altitude on A0: each count is 0.2 cm. Roll on A1: 512 is level, each count is 0.1 degree, positive when the right side is up. Roll gyro on A2: 512 is still, each count is 1 degree per second. Pitch on A3: 512 is level, each count is 0.1 degree, positive when the nose is up. Yaw gyro on A4: 512 is still, each count is 1 degree per second, positive when the nose turns right. Front motors tilt it nose-up when they push harder than the back pair. FL and BR spin one way and FR and BL the other, so speeding up one diagonal pair turns the drone. The drone stays on the pad until the sketch runs.

        Model details
        
      Brushed 12 V motor with a 4 Ω winding and about 3000 rpm at 12 V with no load. The L298N voltage drop is approximated as 1.6 V plus 0.5 Ω times the current. The battery has 0.3 Ω of internal resistance. PWM is averaged, not simulated cycle by cycle. The wheel animation is slowed down so you can see it turn; the rpm readout is the real figure. Holding the wheel locks the shaft. An LED drops 2 V and burns out above 35 mA, and an Arduino pin supplies current through about 25 \u03a9. A potentiometer splits the voltage between its outer pins. Pin voltages in the hover readout are averages. Arduino code is not executed yet, so the pins follow the controls above.
